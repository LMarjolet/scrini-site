When does the next episode come out?

Looking up an air date means opening a search engine, landing on an article from last year, then on a forum talking about a different season. Scrini answers the question for the shows you already follow.

A calendar, not a search

The app gathers the upcoming air dates of your shows and lays them out week by week. You're not hunting for a date: you look at what's coming and you know what the next two weeks look like.

The calendar also shows each show's release schedule. That's what's most often missing elsewhere, and what costs you the most when you don't know it: a season can switch from weekly episodes to dropping all at once, or take a two-month break in the middle. Seeing the schedule means understanding why nothing has come out for three weeks, without having to check whether you missed something.

An alert on the day

On the morning of a release, a notification shows up on your device. No extra email to sort through, no feed to keep an eye on: the information reaches you on the day it's useful, and not before.

If they get in your way, you can turn them all off in one go. That's on purpose: an app that makes unsubscribing a pain ends up uninstalled, which does nobody any good.

What the home screen can show

Upcoming releases are one of the home screen rows, and you choose which rows appear and in what order. Depending on how you set it up, the app opens on your lists, on the releases you're waiting for, on what's trending today, on series currently airing, or on movies now in theaters in your country. If the calendar is what you care about, it can be the first thing you see.

Where the dates come from

Air schedules come from The Movie Database, a database maintained by a community of contributors. It's good, and it isn't infallible: a date that's announced and then moved by a network sometimes takes a day or two to be corrected there, and niche shows are less well kept up than big productions. The app shows what the database contains — no more, no better.
