Privacy Policy

Scrini app — last updated: November 2026

This is a translation. The French version of this policy is the reference.

Scrini stores the movies and TV shows you track, and what it needs to work. No audience measurement, no ad trackers, no selling of your data. This page describes what is kept, where, and how to make it disappear.

Who processes your data

The Scrini app is published by Louis Marjolet, the data controller under the GDPR. Contact: contact@scrini.fr.

The data you give us

Email addressTo create your account, sign you back in, and tie your library to you rather than to a device.
Your name and the address of your profile picture — only if you sign in with Google or AppleWe don't use them. This information comes with the sign-in and is kept as is by the authentication service: the app doesn't read it, doesn't display it anywhere and doesn't pass it on to anyone.

The legal basis for this processing is the performance of the service you request by creating an account. Without this data, the app has no purpose.

Error reports

When something breaks and the app recovers on its own — a data source that's unavailable, a list that won't load — it logs a trace of it: where it happened, the error message, the app version and the operating system. Otherwise, a permanent outage would be impossible to tell apart from normal operation.

These records are linked to your account, so we can tell an outage that affects one person from one that affects everyone. They are deleted after thirty days, and immediately when the account is deleted. Any web addresses quoted in an error message have their parameters stripped before being written: a failed search doesn't leave behind what you typed.

This is neither audience measurement nor usage tracking: nothing is logged when everything works. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in keeping the service running.

Counting catalog requests

Movie and TV show pages go through a relay server (see TMDB below). So that a single account can't use up everyone's access, this relay counts, per account and per window of a few minutes, the number of requests it forwards — a number, nothing about their content. This count is deleted every night. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in keeping the service available to everyone.

What never leaves your phone

Your chosen theme, whether release alerts are turned on, and cached images stay on the device. Notifications are local: the app schedules them with the system, without any token or identifier being sent to a third-party notification service.

What we don't collect

  • No audience measurement, no behavioral analytics tools.
  • No trackers or advertising identifiers on our part. Only Google may store information on your device, when you start a trailer or open the web version — see below.
  • No location data.
  • No access to your contacts, your files or your camera.

Where your data is hosted

The database and the authentication service are hosted by Supabase, in the eu-west-3 (Paris, France) region: your library and your account are stored on servers located in France. Supabase is an American company, whose data infrastructure here is in Europe.

The exceptions, which leave the European Union, are the delivery of sign-in emails and the Google services you use — Google sign-in, YouTube trailers, the web version — as explained below.

Each account is isolated at the database level itself: the server's security rules only allow a row to be read and written by the account that created it.

Third-party services

The Movie Database (TMDB)

Movie and TV show pages come from TMDB. Your requests go through a relay server that queries TMDB without passing on your identity: TMDB receives the search, never who made it. Streaming availability comes from JustWatch, via TMDB. This app uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.

Resend (sending emails)

Sign-in codes are sent to you by Resend, an American company, which receives your email address and the code for that purpose. It has no access to your library or to the rest of your data.

Signing in with Google or Apple

If you choose to sign in with a Google or Apple account, that provider knows you're signing in to Scrini and sends us your email address, your name and — for Google — the address of your profile picture. None of the app's features ask for the last two. They're kept with your account, without being read or displayed. We never receive your password or the contents of your account with them. Signing in with a code sent by email avoids this step entirely.

YouTube (trailers)

Trailers play in YouTube's embedded player, a Google service, through the YouTube API Services. The player only loads when you start a trailer: until then, YouTube receives nothing. As soon as you start one, Google receives your IP address, your device's language and technical information about your browser or phone, and may store or read information on your device (cookies, local storage), even in privacy-enhanced mode. Scrini passes on neither your identity nor your library. By starting a trailer, you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service; what Google does with this data is governed by its Privacy Policy. To keep anything from reaching it, simply don't start a trailer.

Web version: Google services

On the web version (app.scrini.fr), the sign-in page loads the “Sign in with Google” button from Google's servers as soon as it appears, whether you use it or not. Google then receives your IP address and information about your browser; if you are already signed in to a Google account in that browser, it can also read its own cookies and learn that you opened Scrini's sign-in page. The web version's rendering engine and its font are also downloaded from Google's servers, which receive your IP address in the process. The Android and iOS apps make none of these calls. This processing is governed by Google's Privacy Policy.

How long your data is kept

For as long as your account exists, with two exceptions: the error reports described above delete themselves after thirty days, and the catalog request count every night. There is no archiving: when the account is deleted, all the data described above is erased from the database, with no copy kept.

Reporting a mistake

A wrong title page, a missing poster, an incorrect release date: this data comes from the TMDB catalog and gets fixed on their end, at themoviedb.org. The fix then makes its way into Scrini.

A bug, inappropriate content, personal data shown by mistake or a security flaw: write to contact@scrini.fr. Every report gets a reply within one month, and security reports are handled first.

Your rights

The GDPR gives you the right of access, rectification, erasure, portability and objection. In practice:

  • View and correct: all your data is visible and editable right in the app.
  • Get your data: your library can be exported as a spreadsheet from the app, without going through us.
  • Erase: see the account deletion page.
  • Write to us for any other request, at the contact address given above. Reply within one month.

You can also lodge a complaint with the CNIL (the French data protection authority) or with the data protection authority in your country.

Children

The app is not intended for children under 13 and does not knowingly ask them for any data.

Changes

Any change to this policy will be published on this page, with its update date. A change affecting the kind of data collected will be announced in the app.
