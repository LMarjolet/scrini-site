Track your anime

Filed in with TV shows, anime gets lost: three hundred episodes on one side, six on the other, and a list where you can't find anything anymore. Scrini treats anime as the category it is.

Its own filter, because it's its own thing

Anime isn't movies and it isn't TV shows, and mixing them makes a library impossible to read. In Scrini, anime has its own filter — in search as well as in your own lists. So you can look at just your anime, or set it aside while you decide between two shows.

Filters that stack

The anime filter combines with the others instead of replacing them: genre, minimum rating, decades, upcoming releases. “Sci-fi anime from the 1990s above a certain rating” is a question you ask in a few taps, and the same combination works on your list just like on the catalog.

Decades deserve a special mention: it's the filter that makes searching for anime bearable, because the medium has very distinct eras, and sorting by relevance alone always surfaces the same recent titles.

Tracking works the same

Beyond that, you track an anime just like a series: you check off the episodes you've watched, the home screen shows you the next one, and progress only counts what has actually aired — which matters all the more for a season that's being simulcast. Upcoming episodes show up in the calendar, with a notification on the morning of release day.

What to know about the data

Title pages come from The Movie Database, which isn't a database specialized in Japanese animation. In practice, well-known titles are well kept up there, but the way a long-running series is split into seasons can differ from the one used by specialized databases, and fine distinctions — season, arc, part — aren't always reflected. Better to know that before pouring in a list of two hundred titles.

The platforms offering each title in the country or region set in the app are shown when the information exists, based on JustWatch data provided through TMDB.
