Keep track of where you are in your TV shows

You stopped a show in April and pick it back up in October: you play a random episode, realize you've already seen it, try another one. Here's how Scrini saves you that wasted fifteen minutes.

Check it off instead of remembering

It all comes down to one gesture: you check off the episodes you've watched as you go. The app doesn't guess anything, doesn't read what you're watching and doesn't connect to any platform — it records what you tell it, and that's it. In return, the information is accurate, even when you've watched on three different services or at someone else's place.

On the home screen, each series in progress shows you the next episode: the one you need to play, not the one that aired this week. The question “wait, where was I?” goes away because it's already answered when you open the app.

Progress that doesn't count upcoming episodes

It's one of those details you only appreciate once you have it. When a season is still airing, most trackers show your progress against the announced total: you've watched the six episodes out of ten that are out, so you're told 60% and an unfinished season. That's wrong — you're caught up.

Scrini only counts episodes that have actually aired. A series you follow week by week therefore shows as finished as long as there's nothing new, and goes back to behind the day a new episode drops. The difference between “I have four episodes left” and “there aren't any yet” changes what you do with your evening.

Find where to watch it

Once you know which episode is next, you still need to know where it's streaming. Each title shows the platforms that offer it in the country or region set in the app, along with its cast. This information comes from JustWatch, via TMDB: it's only as good as their data, and an exclusive that changes hands can take a few days to show up there.

Your list belongs to you

Everything you check off can be exported as a spreadsheet from “Export my data” in Settings, anytime, with no request to make and no waiting. That's the guarantee that matters when you trust an app with years of viewing: you can leave with it. The rest — what's kept, where, and for how long — is described in the privacy policy.
